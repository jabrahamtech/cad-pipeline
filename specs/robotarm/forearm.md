# forearm — spec

Component `robotarm_forearm`, generated by `models/robotarm/forearm.py`.
Styled per `_style.md`. Gated by `models/robotarm/_check_wrist.py`.

Local frame: origin on the **J4 pitch axis**. +X along that axis, +Y forward
toward the hand, +Z up. The J3 elbow axis is at y = −`fa_l2`.

## Function

The link between the elbow and the wrist, 40 mm long, carrying **both**
wrist servos. It forks at both ends: the upper arm's elbow block sits
between its rear prongs, the roll housing between its front prongs, and a
`wrist_hub` journals in each of the four bores.

**It forks at both ends because it cannot do anything else.** Straddled at
the elbow and forking at the wrist, its inboard material would have to live
at y < −25.4 (clear of the roll housing's swing) and its outboard material
at y > −25.8 (clear of the upper arm's prongs). Those two regions do not
overlap, and nothing inside the 0.4 mm running gap can bridge them. Forking
at both ends puts the side walls at 12.4 < |x| < 19.9 for the whole length,
unbroken, and the problem disappears.

**The joint clearance is cut, not drawn.** Two cylinders — `wr_sweep_r`
about J4, `fa_elbow_clear` about J3 — plus the half-space behind J3 are
subtracted from everything inside the straddle. What survives on the
centreline is an hourglass: 3.2 mm wide where the two swings nearly meet,
wider above and below where they do not. Drawing that outline by hand would
be a guess that goes stale the moment either neighbour changes size.

J4's clearance is a disc because the roll housing only swings. J3's is a
disc **and** everything behind it, because the upper arm does not swing
about the elbow, it continues through it. Cutting only the disc left the
elbow end webbed across and the fork shut — caught by eye in the render,
not by any assertion, which is a gap in the gate.

## Interfaces — the dimensions that matter

| what | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| `fa_l2` | 40.0 | J3→J4. Sets reach 125 mm at 25 g — `_assembly.md` |
| `wr_straddle` | 24.0 | SHARED. What the front fork straddles = `wrist_roll` |
| `fa_gap` | 0.40 | free fit, plate inner face to the straddled part |
| `fa_plate_in` | 12.4 | derived, `wr_straddle`/2 + `fa_gap` |
| `fa_plate_t` | 7.5 | derived, `wr_journal` 5.0 + `fa_cbore_depth` 2.5 |
| `fa_bore_d` | 20.35 | derived, `wr_hub_d` + rotating 0.35 |
| `fa_cbore_d` | 24.35 | derived, `wr_flange_d` + rotating 0.35 |
| `fa_cbore_extra` | 0.50 | the flange must NEVER seat — see below |
| `fa_boss_r` | 13.775 | derived, `fa_cbore_d`/2 + wall |
| `fa_waist_t` | 3.5 | wall between the bosses. Biggest single lever on mass |
| `fa_fillet_r` | 30.0 | the concave arc between the bosses IS the silhouette |
| `fa_elbow_clear` | 11.4 | what the upper arm's elbow block sweeps about J3 |
| `fa_lobe_t` | 4.0 | derived, MG90S `tab_below_top` 1.0 + `tab_thk` 3.0 |
| `fa_pilot_d` | 1.8 | servo screw pilot, self-tapping into PETG |

**The counterbore is deeper than the flange on purpose.** The hex bottoming
in its socket is what locates the joint axially. If the flange seated too,
two hard stops would fight each other across four stacked tolerances. 0.5 mm
of deliberate slack decides which stop wins.

**Silhouette by tangency.** Two circles of `fa_boss_r` joined by two concave
arcs of `fa_fillet_r`, each tangent to both. Waist half-height falls out at
8.94 mm — derived, never typed. There is no straight edge anywhere on the
side view and therefore no corner to fillet afterwards and get subtly wrong.

## Loads & environment

Carries the entire wrist chain plus payload: 0.908 kg·cm about J3 against
the elbow MG90S's 1.00 kg·cm duty limit, **91 %**. Empty, 55 %. The part's
own contribution is 27.6 g·cm — 3 % of the total. Lightening it further is
not where the margin is; see the table in `_assembly.md`.

The J4 servo's 13.4 g hangs 12 mm outboard of the +X wall on a 3.5 mm waist.
Bending there is trivial; the case in its window carries the shear.

## Cables & service

`fa_loom_d` 9.0 bore on the centreline through the web, taking the 4-lead
bundle from the upper arm. Each servo's own lead exits its case inside its
lobe. The J5 lead crosses J4 through the idler hub's own 5.5 screw bore, on
the pitch axis, where it does not flex — that bore exists for the driver and
is free.

**Not yet designed:** lead retention, and the channel from the lobe windows
into the loom bore. Both wait on the forearm cover.

## Print plan

−X face down, built along +X, PETG. All four journal bores are then vertical
and round — they are the only precision fits in the part.

**This needs support** under the +X wall's bosses and both lobes. No
orientation of a fork carrying two servo mounts is support-free: whichever
face is on the bed, the opposite wall's outline grows relative to what is
under it. A sagging 20 mm journal is the worse trade. Split the part on the
XZ plane if the support scarring on the +X face matters.

## Acceptance

Gated by `_check_wrist.py`, which rebuilds the chain and sweeps pitch
−90…+90 and roll −90…+90:

- interference across forearm, both J4 hubs, roll housing and rotator: **0.000 mm³** at every pose
- `roll^forearm` minimum distance: **0.400** at every pose, exactly the contracted running gap
- `hub^forearm` and `hub^roll`: **0.000**, and that is the contract being met — those are the two seating faces that trap the hub without a screw

Before printing: the **rack/pinion mesh gauge**, the **MG90S tab-plate
gauge** (29.0 pitch, *not* the SG90's 27.8), and a **hub-in-bore gauge** —
the hex is 18.24 across corners and is threaded through a 20.35 bore, which
is 1.06 mm of diametral clearance and the tightest assembly move in the arm.

## ASSUMED / unverified

- `fa_elbow_clear` 11.4 is a **forward reference**: the upper arm's elbow block does not exist yet. If it comes out bigger, the centreline web (3.2 mm) is what gets eaten, and `fa_l2` has to grow.
- MG90S case and tab dimensions are DATASHEET, not measured. The tab plane is the datum, so a fat case costs nothing; a wrong tab pitch costs the lobe.
- Printed mass taken as 0.6 × solid PETG throughout. Not yet weighed.
- Support-free print orientation is asserted nowhere and checked by nobody.
